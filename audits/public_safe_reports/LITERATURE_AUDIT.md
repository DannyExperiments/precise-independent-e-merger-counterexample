## Exact question and model

The version of record of Vovk and Wang, Electronic Journal of Statistics 18 (2024), pp. 1185–1205, defines Borel independent e-mergers in Section 2. Equations (18)–(19) in Section 6 allow starting capital in $[0,1]$, predictable fractions in $[0,1]$, and any measurable unread-coordinate selection using the previously seen values. The strategy law is fixed across inputs and can couple the reading and betting rules. Theorem 3 proves validity of every such generalized martingale merging function. Question 4 on printed p. 1203 asks for representation of precise ie-mergers. Coordinatewise increasing domination is a separate Question 3 on the same page. ## Prior counterexamples and general facts

- Vovk and Wang's Example 4, credited to Zhenyuan Zhang, already shows that an ie-merger on two inputs can evade pointwise domination by generalized martingale mixtures. It is nonmonotone and not precise. For its threshold $h>1$, take the first input constant 1 and the second to be $h$ with probability $1/h$ and 0 otherwise. The expected output is $1-1/h<1$ even though both means equal one. [Journal PDF, Example 4, p. 1202](https://pure.royalholloway.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/61949496/24-EJS2228.pdf#page=18)
- Ramdas and Wang's monograph uses the term *exact* for mean-one validity. Proposition 8.6 explicitly treats admissibility of exact ie-mergers without monotonicity. Its proof uses positive mass at an arbitrary point under some product distribution of exact e-variables. The release uses this prior observation to pass from nonrepresentation of its particular exact merger to nondomination. [Monograph, Proposition 8.6, p. 116](https://web.stanford.edu/~aramdas/ebook-final.pdf#page=129)
- Vovk and Wang's Example 2 and Ramdas and Wang's Example 8.8 use $f(x,y)=\frac12(\frac{x}{1+x}+\frac{y}{1+y})(1+xy)$. It is precise and outside the class of convex mixtures of fixed subset products. It lies inside the generalized martingale class. Averaging the two reading orders, bet all capital on the first value $x$ and then use the fraction $x/(1+x)$ on the second input $y$. The resulting wealth is $x(1+xy)/(1+x)$. The sequential restricted result therefore does not give a Q570 counterexample. [Journal Example 2](https://pure.royalholloway.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/61949496/24-EJS2228.pdf#page=17) · [Monograph Example 8.8, p. 117](https://web.stanford.edu/~aramdas/ebook-final.pdf#page=130)

## Exact overlap in the Boolean selector

The map $(a_1,a_2,a_3)\mapsto ((1-a_2)a_3,(1-a_3)a_1,(1-a_1)a_2)$ on bits is the known Lugano or Araújo–Feix/Baumeler–Wolf process. See Baumeler and Wolf's 2016 Example 4 and Table I, with attribution to Araújo and Feix in reference 56. The equations are explicit in Kunjwal and Baumeler's 2023 Eq. (2) and in Dourdent et al.'s Eq. (2). The known noncausality results concern the whole multibit process and associated causal games. The present test-merger statement concerns a scalar output and the precise wealth protocol of Vovk and Wang. [Baumeler and Wolf (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1088/1367-2630/18/1/013036) · [Example 4 and truth table](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1507.01714#page=12) · [Kunjwal and Baumeler (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.131.120201) · [Dourdent et al., Eq. (2)](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.23599#S1)

There is a direct prior implication for **precision** of the hard-threshold readout. Each factor is the unchanged law of the local bit when the process flag is 0 and its size-biased law when the flag is 1. Under mean-one inputs both are stochastic kernels. The logical-consistency normalization $\sum_a\prod_i P_i(a_i\mid w_i(a))=1$ then gives the expected product of the selected inputs, which equals the merger since at most one selector is active. This is an inference from the published process normalization, not a literature claim that those papers define an e-merger. The release proves validity and precision directly, including a continuous cutoff, and uses scalar values at four points to rule out the specified strategy class.

## Related updates and search limits

Wang's arbitrary-dependence theorem and Clerico's geometric extension beyond monotonicity assume validity for all joint laws, including dependent laws. Their dominating weighted averages are not a complete-class result for independent inputs. Clerico's November 2026 sequential preprint concerns conditional testing constraints and e-processes. Ming et al.'s October 2026 version of *Combining e-values using demi-supermartingales* proves tail bounds (Theorems 3.3 and 5.2), rather than representation of precise ie-mergers.
